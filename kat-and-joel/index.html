Kat & Joel

are getting married (soon to be the Thompsons), with every detail handled by Dream Details Wedding Co., and one very late guest on the logistics committee.

A fictional wedding, from a novel in progress by Lucy Hartfield

Our story

We met in college. Joel was treasurer of the robotics team, and Kat was president of the student senate. Joel petitioned the senate for extra funding so the team could offer travel scholarships to members who couldn't otherwise afford to join. Kat was won over by the thoroughness of his funding proposal, and by the typo on the last slide that said "tank you for your time."

Six years and one dog named Tank later, we're saying "I do."

Questions

Does a Friday wedding actually save money?
Twelve to eighteen percent, typically. Venues discount off-peak days to fill dead inventory. Catering usually stays flat. (Nate answered this one before Lila could.)
Which cake did you pick?
The tasting included traditional, carrot, marbled with raspberry filling, and red velvet. Kat is still deciding. Please don't rush her.
Will the bald eagles be a problem during cocktail hour?
Nate asked. Lila had already asked. Cara, the venue coordinator, insists there have never been any incidents.
Where is Uncle Joe sitting?
Table six. Or seven. Nate and Lila are still discussing it.
Why so many florals along the aisle?
Nate measured. Lila won. Gramma Sue gets her own usher, so everybody wins.
Can we bring a plus-one?
Only with more than fifteen minutes' notice. Lila would like that on the record.
Is this a real wedding?
No. Kat, Joel, Lila, and Nate live in Lucy Hartfield's debut rom-com, which is being written right now. Your RSVP joins her newsletter, where the real wedding details (and the sneak peeks) show up first.

Nate, please stop measuring the aisle.
